Macroeconomic Outlook in Brief

Global: Uncertainty Weighs on Growth

Uncertainty regarding the war in Iran is pushing energy prices further up and is likely to weigh further on growth.

USA: Private Consumption is Stronger Than Previously Assumed

The recent data revision shows that US households have higher incomes than previously assumed. However, rising financing costs increase growth risks.

Eurozone: Recovery Driven by Germany

Business conditions are recovering mainly thanks to Germany, while France remains weak.

China: Government Supports Weak Domestic Economy

Expansionary monetary and fiscal policies stabilize the economy, but weak domestic demand and geopolitical risks act as a brake.

The Underrated US Consumer

REAL ECONOMY

Growth

USA and Eurozone: Improvement in Business Conditions

In September, business conditions in the US and the Eurozone improved further. The Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) rose to 53.1 in the Eurozone and surged further to 58.4 in the US. In China, the picture remained split: the official NBS PMI rose slightly above the expansion threshold to 50.7, while the private export-focused RatingDog-PMI gained to 52.4.

… Particularly in the Services Sector

In the US, the services sector gained significant further momentum. The PMI rose to 58.8, driven especially by positive prospects in the technology sector. Business conditions in the Eurozone services sector improved in September with a PMI of 53.0. Manufacturing also gained ground, reaching 52.9.

  • USA
  • Eurozone

USA: Robust New Orders Support Growth Outlook

New orders continue to signal positive momentum for the US economy. In September, new orders in the services sector continued to rise strongly. In manufacturing, the PMI for new orders rose further to 55.3.

US Growth: Driven by Private Consumption

Recent data revisions slightly increased US GDP growth from 2.11% to 2.19% year-on-year. Private consumption was the key growth driver, contributing 1.72 percentage points. Investments in hardware and software, including a large share of AI infrastructure, contributed 1.0 percentage points. Because many hardware components are imported, their growth contribution is likely significantly lower. Whether AI investments are truly driving growth remains questionable.

Germany Recovers

In Germany and France, business conditions improved in September. In Germany, the Composite PMI rose to 53.8. Manufacturing remained the main driver, with the services sector PMI at 52.9 returning to expansionary territory above 50. New orders are predominantly coming from defense and the AI industry. In France, conditions brightened. The Composite PMI rose to 51.1, supported by a recovery in the services sector (51.2).

  • Germany
  • France

German Industrial Production Barely Recovers

The truck toll mileage index, which tracks toll-paying heavy commercial vehicle traffic on highways and serves as a leading indicator for industrial production, has noticeably recovered after a prolonged period of weakness since 2024, but has been moving sideways for months and shows barely any growth on an annual average. Industrial production follows this trend, declining less sharply than in 2025.

Spain and Italy: Business Conditions Remain Expansionary

In Spain and Italy, business conditions remained expansionary in September. In Spain, the Composite PMI rose to 56.8 points, supported by the services sector (58.3), while conditions in manufacturing improved slightly again. In Italy, business conditions continued to improve, albeit somewhat more slowly than in the previous month.

  • Spain
  • Italy

China: Weak Domestic Economy

The Purchasing Managers’ Indices for September 2026 point to a slight recovery in China’s domestic economy. The official NBS PMI rose slightly above the expansion threshold in both manufacturing (50.1) and services (50.2). The private RatingDog-PMI gained further to 52.1 in manufacturing and 51.6 in services.

Construction Activity Fails to Recover

The number of new housing starts remained weak in August as well and has persisted far below 2010 levels since 2022. This is also reflected in the numbers of unfinished and completed properties, which continue to show no sign of a turnaround.

Financial Conditions

USA and Eurozone: Rising Financing Costs for Companies

Financing costs for companies have recently risen in both the US and the Eurozone. Yields on five-year corporate bonds increased in both regions for both AAA and BBB bonds. The yield spreads between credit rating categories remained largely stable.

  • us_corp_yields
  • ez_corp_yields

US Mortgage Rates Rise Again

Financing costs for residential real estate are developing differently in the US and Germany. In the US, interest rates for 30-year mortgages recently rose significantly to 7.4%. In Germany, by contrast, interest rates for new residential construction loans remained largely stable at around 4.0%. Rising financing costs could weigh on demand for residential real estate in the US.

Weak Credit Impulse

The credit impulse (the change in credit flow to the private sector in percentage points of GDP) is a good leading indicator for the development of private demand. Recently, it was slightly positive in both the US and the Eurozone, but lost momentum. Since the number of banks reporting rising lending and credit demand is declining significantly, particularly in the Eurozone, this points to an impending negative credit impulse.

  • USA
  • Eurozone

Private Consumption

USA: Retail Sales Continue to Rise

Retail sales in the US continue to grow solidly. The weekly Johnson Redbook Index rose further to 10.4% year-on-year in early October, pointing to continued robust nominal consumption among US households.

USA: Credit Card Debt Continues to Rise as Usual

Credit card debt grew in recent weeks similarly to the average since 2012. The monthly change recently aligned with the typical seasonal pattern of the same calendar weeks in previous years.

USA: Google Searches Point to More Cautious Consumption

Consumption often starts with an internet search. Online search data therefore serves as a good leading indicator for private consumption. Search intensity for the term “restaurant” in the US in recent weeks was slightly below the average of the last five years, having previously been higher. Terms in the “travel” category have been searched somewhat less than the five-year average since March.

  • Restaurant
  • Travel

USA: The US Savings Rate is Higher Than Expected

Between 2000 and 2019, the US household savings rate averaged 5.2%. Following the recent data revision, the savings rate in July was not at 3% as previously assumed, but significantly higher at 4.6%. The savings rate had been underestimated since 2022. The US consumer therefore has significantly more cushion than thought just a month ago. In August 2026, the savings rate declined from 4.6% in July to 4.1%.

Republican Consumer Sentiment Turns

American consumer sentiment remains on average pessimistic. The Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index is at levels seen during the COVID crisis. The University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index remains at levels otherwise only seen during recessions. As usual, supporters of the opposition party are significantly more pessimistic. Republicans, by contrast, remained optimistic, but have become more pessimistic since the outbreak of the war in Iran.

  • Sentiment
  • Partisanship

Consumer Restraint in France and Germany

Google search queries for “restaurant” in Germany and France point to weak and declining private demand. In both countries, search intensity in recent weeks was lower than the five-year average for the same time of year.

  • Germany
  • France

Less Caution in Spain and Italy

While search intensity for “restaurant” in Spain remained as high as the average of recent years, it was slightly higher in Italy.

  • Spain
  • Italy

China: Sentiment Remains Pessimistic

In China, consumer sentiment has improved slightly since early 2025, but remains historically low. Sentiment in the real estate sector is likewise depressed. The “National Real Estate Climate Index” has been trending downward for three years. Publication of the index was discontinued in January on the grounds that it no longer accurately represented actual developments in the real estate sector.

Labor Market

Slower Rise in Nominal Wages

In September, average hourly earnings in the US rose by 3.0% year-on-year, roughly as fast as in the previous month. In the second quarter of 2026, wage growth was 4.0% in Germany and 1.6% in France.

USA: Normalization of Wage Growth?

Wage growth according to the Indeed Hiring Lab Wage Tracker leads US median wage growth by about eight months. In August, growth in posted wages was stable at 2.5%, while actual wage growth stood at 4.1% in August.

USA: Stable Wage Growth Across All Income Groups

In September, average hourly earnings in the US rose by 3.7%. In the lowest wage quartile, they rose by 3.4%, somewhat slower than in the previous month, and remained unchanged at 3.8% in the highest quartile. Overall, wage growth across all four wage quartiles ranges between 3.4% and 4.0%.

The US Labor Market is Less Tight

The Beveridge curve shows the relationship between the job vacancy rate—vacancies as a percentage of total employment plus vacancies—and the unemployment rate. During the COVID crisis, the curve shifted outward because policy measures kept unemployment high despite abundant job openings. These rigidities have dissipated, and the Beveridge curve has returned to pre-COVID levels. The US labor market environment has thus normalized.

US Job Growth Remains Stable

In September, non-farm private sector employment rose by 0.6% year-on-year according to the ADP report. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) figures showed annual growth of 0.5% (unchanged from the previous month). The US labor market remains stable, but job growth is now proceeding more slowly than in the years between the financial and COVID crises.

USA: Layoffs and Initial Claims Low

In September, announced job cuts according to Challenger fell again to around 43,300 (after 52,900 in August). At the same time, weekly initial jobless claims remained at a low level of around 197,000 at the end of September.

  • Layoffs
  • Initial Claims

Nominal Wages Rising Faster in Germany

The Indeed Hiring Lab Wage Tracker shows a noticeable acceleration in nominal wage growth in August. In the Eurozone, the growth rate rose to 3.1%, and in Germany even to 3.5%. In France, wage growth picked up to 1.8%, while it remained stable at 2.5% in the US.

Eurozone: Tight Labor Market

The Beveridge curve, illustrating the relationship between vacancy rate and unemployment rate, points to a tight labor market in the Eurozone. During the COVID crisis, the curve shifted outward as policy measures caused unemployment to remain elevated despite high job vacancies. In the meantime, these rigidities have largely dissipated and unemployment stands at historically low levels. Although the vacancy rate has declined, it once again aligns with the historical relationship between the two variables.

China: Very High Youth Unemployment

China’s unemployment rate moved sideways at around 5% in recent years and rose slightly to 5.3% in August. Youth unemployment, defined as the jobless rate among 16- to 24-year-olds, reached 21.3% in mid-2023. Following a six-month publication suspension, a new statistic “excluding students” was introduced. After a previous decline, this rate rose further to 18.9% in August.

PRICES

Commodities

Ocean Freight Costs Continue to Rise

Freight rates for crude oil tankers have exploded to new record highs. They lie far above the peak level of April following the start of the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. The reasons are longer routes, detours, and waiting times that tie up ships and tighten the available supply of tankers. The World Container Index (WCI), which tracks freight rates in goods trade, has also surged in recent weeks due to high fuel costs and geopolitical uncertainties on sea routes.

Oil Price Shock Lasts Longer Than in 2022

Futures prices for oil deliveries in one year have risen further in recent weeks and now stand at their highest level since the start of the war. In 2022, after the outbreak of the war in Ukraine, oil futures rose similarly sharply, but had normalized again by this time of year. While futures markets for crude oil price in lower oil prices in one year than in one month, they are significantly higher than before the outbreak of the war in Iran.

  • Forward Curve

When Will Shipping Through the Strait of Hormuz Normalize?

A reopening of the Strait of Hormuz is decisive for the oil supply. Following renewed attacks, prediction markets price in a reopening by the end of October with a probability of only around 1%. For the end of November, the probability stands at around 6%, and for the end of December at around 14%.

Volatile European Gas Prices

Europe remains heavily dependent on oil and gas imports. Following an interim sideways movement, Dutch TTF front-month natural gas futures picked up again recently and stand almost 150% above levels prior to the start of the war in Iran. During the 2022 energy crisis, European gas prices temporarily tripled. By this time of year, however, they had already fallen sharply after the missing Russian supply had largely been replaced by LNG imports.

USA

USA: Inflation Rate Remains High

In September, consumer prices in the US continued to rise noticeably. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased by 3.7% year-on-year, slightly exceeding the consensus expectation of 3.6%. Core inflation rose to 2.6%, also somewhat higher than the 2.5% expected by participants in a monthly Bloomberg survey.

  • Headline CPI
  • Core CPI

Indices Point to Elevated Inflation

Inflation is measured using various indices: the CPI captures consumer prices, the PCE index tracks household consumption expenditures, and the GDP deflator reflects prices of all domestically produced goods and services. Inflation rates for all three indicators peaked in mid-2022. In August 2026, the annual inflation rate of the Fed’s preferred Core PCE Index (excluding food and energy) stood at 3.0%. The new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh takes into account, among others, “trimmed-mean inflation,” which excludes the largest price increases and decreases. Under this measure, inflation in August ranged between 2.2% and 2.6%.

Broad-Based Inflation in the PCE Basket

In his Jackson Hole keynote address (August 2026), Fed Chair Kevin Warsh underscored the persistent breadth of inflationary pressures by pointing to the 199 components of the PCE basket: In August 2026, 52.0% of the recorded goods and services registered price increases exceeding 3% year-on-year. While this value is below the post-pandemic peak (around 77%), it remains above the level of the two pre-crisis decades (2000–2019 average: 31.9%). On a six-month annualized basis, the share of components with inflation rates above 3% recently moderated noticeably to 42.4%.

Inflation Drivers: Services and Energy

Services inflation remains the dominant driver of US inflation. While the food price contribution remains moderately positive, the recent oil price shock is now feeding through in full force: energy’s contribution to inflation is at its highest level since 2022, pushing headline inflation noticeably higher once again.

USA: Rising Inflationary Pressure

In September, services prices increased by 3.2% year-on-year. Food prices picked up at an annual rate of 1.8%. Energy prices rose particularly sharply, surging by 20.4% compared to the previous year.

USA: Supercore Inflation Rises Less Strongly

Supercore is defined as the services price index excluding energy and housing costs. Based on the CPI index, the annual supercore inflation rate in September was around 3.1%. Measured by the PCE index, supercore inflation stood at around 3.5% in August. This shows that inflationary pressure in the services sector remains high even without housing costs.

Rent Growth Has Stopped Falling

Rental costs make up a large share of services prices in the CPI and are also strongly correlated with owners’ equivalent rent. During the pandemic years, the Zillow rent index for new leases was a reliable leading indicator for rent trends, as existing leases adjust with a lag. Currently, the Zillow rent index at around 2.7% signals similar rent growth as the CPI rent index at approximately 2.8%.

USA: Inflation Could Rise Further

The prices paid index in the ISM Services PMI leads consumer price inflation by approximately six months. In September, the ISM price index rose further to 74.0 points, indicating heightened inflationary pressure.

Eurozone

Rising Inflation in the Eurozone

In September, Eurozone HICP rose further. Year-on-year, HICP climbed to 3.8%, while core inflation stood at 2.5%. In Germany (3.3%), France (3.4%), Italy (4.1%), and Spain (5.0%), HICP accelerated once again.

Inflationary Pressure is Rising

Until late 2022, inflation was primarily driven by energy and food prices. Currently, price increases are predominantly driven by rising services and energy prices.

Inflation Expectations

Fed Forecasts vs. Reality

The US Federal Reserve assumes that inflation in the US will not return to the 2% target until 2029. At the beginning of the year, 2028 was still being projected. Since the resurgence of inflation in 2021, the Fed has had to revise its forecast year after year and postpone reaching the target once again.

Volatile Consumer Inflation Expectations

In September 2026, US consumer inflation expectations for the next twelve months jumped to 4.6% (University of Michigan; New York Fed in August: 3.9%). In the Eurozone, households expected a median of 3.0% for the coming year and 2.9% for the next three years according to the ECB in August.

  • USA
  • Eurozone

Energy Becomes More Expensive Again …

Food and energy prices are excluded to analyze the underlying inflation trend. For the perception of purchasing power and voting behavior, however, precisely these prices are crucial. After the sharp rise in 2021, energy prices had stabilized, but food prices continue to rise. The war in Iran has now driven energy costs up once again: in the US, they stand 24% above levels prior to Trump’s election (Nov 2024), and 17% in the Eurozone.

  • USA
  • Eurozone

… Particularly Diesel …

Diesel prices, the primary fuel for road freight transport, have risen sharply again in recent weeks and are almost twice as high as before the start of the war in Iran.

… Which is Likely to Hurt US Republicans

In November 2026, the entire House of Representatives and 35 of the 100 Senate seats will be up for reelection. In prediction markets, a Republican majority in the House is currently priced in with only around 9.5%. For the Senate, the probability has recently fallen significantly and currently stands at around 36.5%.

INTEREST RATE EXPECTATIONS & FISCAL IMPULSE

USA: Rising Real Interest Rates

The real interest rate is the difference between the nominal interest rate and (expected) inflation. While the nominal interest rate can be observed in the market, an estimate must be used for expected inflation. Depending on the chosen approach, the (expected) real interest rate for the US economy currently lies in positive territory between 0.9% and 2.9%. Alternatively, one can observe the yield on inflation-indexed government bonds (TIPS in the US) in the market. For a two-year maturity, the real rate has been positive since April 2022 and recently rose to around 2.5%.

Volatile Interest Rate Expectations

Futures markets are pricing in rate hikes of a good 25 basis points for the US Federal Reserve through January 2027. Through July 2027, an additional nearly 50 basis points are priced in. This would bring the key policy rate back close to levels prior to last year’s rate cuts. For the Eurozone, further rate hikes totaling around 60 basis points are priced in through July 2027, of which roughly 20 to 25 basis points by January 2027. Expected interest rates in Europe remain significantly below those in the US.

  • Fed (US)
  • ECB (Eurozone)

China: Expansionary Monetary Policy

The People’s Bank of China manages monetary policy primarily through three tools: the 7-day reverse repo rate, the one-year Loan Prime Rate (LPR) of commercial banks, and the reserve requirement ratio (RRR). In recent years, it cut all three rates repeatedly to inject liquidity and support the economy via credit expansion. For 2026, it signals a continuing moderately loose monetary policy.

Moderate Fiscal Impulse for 2026

The fiscal impulse measures the change in the cyclically adjusted government deficit as a percentage of GDP. Positive values indicate an expansionary fiscal policy, negative values a contractionary one. Compared to its April forecast, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) revised its assessment particularly for the Eurozone and China. In the Eurozone, the impulse in 2026 rises to a good 0.6%, as the German defense and infrastructure program unfolds its effect. For 2027, barely any additional impulse is expected. In China, it weakens to nearly 0.3% in 2026. In the US, the impulse in 2025 turns out slightly positive instead of negative, but more strongly negative in 2027.

USA: The sharp improvement in business conditions coupled with stable private consumption and a solid labor market underpin the positive momentum. On the fiscal side, no budget consolidation is planned. Rather, the stance remains expansionary. Monetarily, multiple interest rate hikes are priced in through mid-2027, which tightens financing conditions.

China: The persistent weakness in the real estate sector weighs on domestic demand. Manufacturing stabilizes the economy via exports, albeit particularly via subsidies, which points to overcapacity and has a deflationary effect. Slightly positive impulses can be expected from fiscal and monetary policy.

Eurozone / Germany: A recovery in Germany supports momentum in the Eurozone, while France remains weak. Spain and Italy contribute to growth, with the services sector providing the main contribution. Debt-financed economic stimulus programs, particularly in Germany, should provide an impulse through late 2026. Without deregulation, however, German infrastructure and defense investments are likely to have only a weak effect. Rising oil and gas prices as well as more restrictive financing conditions place an additional burden on the economy. We have slightly raised our forecast for Germany to 0.7%, while for the Eurozone it remains at 0.6%.
