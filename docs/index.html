Introduction

Macroeconomic Outlook in Brief

Global: The Oil Price Shock Continues

Oil prices have fallen recently, yet the shock continues. High energy and transport costs as well as inflation risks weigh on the global economy.

USA: Growth Risks Are Rising

Private consumption remains the most important growth driver. However, a falling savings rate, high financing costs, and volatile equity markets increase the risks.

Euro Area: Germany Recovers, Inflation Rises Further

Germany is gaining momentum, France remains weak. The renewed rise in inflation complicates a broader recovery.

China: Monetary Policy Supports, Domestic Economy Remains Weak

The real estate crisis and weak consumer sentiment weigh on domestic demand. An expansionary monetary policy attempts to counteract this.

Can the Economy Withstand the Oil Price Shock?

Economic Situation

USA and Eurozone Continue to Expand, but at a Slower Pace

In October, business conditions in the US and the Eurozone remained in expansionary territory, but weakened slightly. The Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) stood at 51.7 in the Eurozone and at 55.9 in the US. In China, the picture remained split: the official NBS PMI dropped just below the neutral threshold at 49.7, while the private export-focused RatingDog-PMI stood at 52.0.

US Consumption Remains the Main Growth Driver

In the third quarter of 2026, real US GDP grew by 2.07% year-on-year (following 2.19% in the second quarter). Private consumption was the main growth driver, contributing 1.7 percentage points. The direct growth contribution of AI-related investments (hardware + software) was a solid 0.9 percentage points, but net imports contributed -1.18 percentage points to growth. Although large sums were invested in IT infrastructure, much of it was purchased abroad. Investments remain heavily concentrated in AI hardware and software. The growth contribution of all other investments was very low at 0.15 percentage points.

Germany Continues to Recover, France Does Not

In Germany, business conditions are improving, while they are deteriorating significantly in France. In Germany, the Composite PMI stood at 53.4. New orders are predominantly coming from defense and the AI industry. In France, by contrast, conditions slumped: the Composite PMI slid to 46.6 points, driven especially by weak business conditions in the services sector. Political instability and high financing costs worsened the outlook for the coming year.

  • Germany
  • France

China: Domestic Economy Remains Weak

The Purchasing Managers’ Indices for October 2026 point to an ongoing fragile condition of China’s domestic economy. The official NBS PMI fell back below the neutral threshold in manufacturing at 49.3 points and stood exactly on the neutral line in services at 50.0 points. The private RatingDog-PMI held up in expansionary territory at 52.0 points in manufacturing and 50.8 points in services.

Oil Price Shock

Oil Price Shock

Oil Prices Fall, but the Shock Persists

Oil prices have fallen noticeably in recent days. Futures prices for oil deliveries in one year also eased, but remain well above their level prior to the start of the war in Iran. In 2022, following a similarly sharp increase, oil futures had already largely normalized by this point in time.

  • Forward Curve
  • Event Study

European Gas Prices Remain Significantly Elevated

Europe remains heavily dependent on oil and gas imports. Following a renewed increase, Dutch TTF front-month natural gas futures eased recently and stand around 170% above the level prior to the start of the war in Iran. During the 2022 energy crisis, European gas prices temporarily tripled. By this time of year, however, they had already fallen sharply after the missing Russian supply had largely been replaced by LNG imports.

More Expensive Energy Weighs on Purchasing Power

Food and energy prices are excluded to analyze the underlying inflation trend. For the perception of purchasing power and voting behavior, however, precisely these prices are crucial. After the sharp rise in 2021, energy prices had stabilized, but food prices continue to rise. The war in Iran has now driven energy costs up once again: in the US, they stand 24% above levels prior to Trump’s election (Nov 2024). In the euro area, they are up 19%.

  • USA
  • Eurozone

Prediction Markets Expect No Quick Reopening of Hormuz

A reopening of the Strait of Hormuz is decisive for the oil supply. Prediction markets price in a reopening by the end of November with a probability of only around 3%. For the end of December, the probability stands at barely 8%, reflecting persistent risk premia in global freight and energy costs.

Inflation and Monetary Policy

Inflation and Monetary Policy

USA

US Inflation Remains High and Exceeds Expectations

In October, consumer prices in the US continued to rise noticeably. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased by 0.4% month-on-month, slightly exceeding the consensus expectation of 0.3%. The CPI excluding food and energy rose by 0.3%, also somewhat higher than the 0.2% expected by participants in a monthly Bloomberg survey. The BLS statistical agency did not publish annual figures because price data was not collected in October of the previous year due to the government shutdown. Compared to September of the previous year, the headline CPI rose by 4.1% and the core CPI by 2.8%.

  • Headline CPI
  • Core CPI

A Different Picture Depending on the Inflation Measure

Inflation is measured using various indices: the CPI captures consumer prices, the PCE index tracks household consumption expenditures, and the GDP deflator reflects prices of all domestically produced goods and services. Inflation rates across all three indicators peaked in mid-2022. In September 2026, the annual inflation rate of the Fed’s preferred Core PCE Index (excluding food and energy) stood at 3.1%. The new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh takes into account, among others, “trimmed-mean inflation,” which excludes the largest price increases and decreases. Under this measure, inflation in September ranged between 2.3% and 2.7%.

For Almost Half of PCE Components, Inflation Exceeds 3%

In his speech at the Jackson Hole symposium (August 2026), Fed Chair Kevin Warsh emphasized the persistent breadth of price pressures and pointed to the components of the PCE basket: In September 2026, 48.6% of tracked goods and services recorded a price increase of more than 3% year-on-year. Although this figure dropped below the 50% mark for the first time in months (post-pandemic peak: around 77%), it remains significantly above the level of the two pre-crisis decades (2000–2019 average: 31.9%). On a six-month annualized basis, the share of components with inflation rates above 3% stood at 44.6% most recently.

Services and Energy Drive US Inflation

Services inflation remains the dominant driver of US inflation. While the contribution of food prices remains moderately positive, the recent oil price shock is now feeding through in full force: the inflation contribution of energy prices is at its highest level since 2022, pushing the headline rate noticeably higher once again.

US Supercore Inflation Remains High

Supercore is defined as the services price index excluding energy and housing costs. Based on the CPI index, the annual rate of supercore inflation rose to around 3.4% in October. Measured by the PCE index, supercore inflation stood at around 3.6% in September. This shows that inflationary pressure in the services sector remains high even without housing costs.

USA: Rents Could Rise Faster Again

Rental costs make up a large share of services prices in the CPI and are also strongly correlated with owners’ equivalent rent. During the pandemic years, the Zillow rent index for new leases was a reliable leading indicator for rent trends, as existing leases adjust with a lag. Currently, the Zillow rent index, at around 2.9%, signals similar rent growth to the CPI rent index at approximately 3.0%, but has been rising for several months.

ISM Prices Signal Ongoing Inflationary Pressure

The prices paid index in the ISM Services PMI leads consumer price inflation by approximately six months. In October, the ISM price index remained at a very high level of 73.0 points, indicating persistent high inflationary pressure.

Eurozone & Expectations

Eurozone Inflation Rises to 4%, Core Rate Remains Stable

In October, Eurozone HICP rose further to 4.0% year-on-year, while core inflation remained at 2.5%. The development across member states was split: while inflation surged to 5.6% in Italy and to 3.8% in France, it dropped to 2.8% in Germany.

  • HICP & Core
  • Country Comparison

Energy and Services Drive Eurozone Inflation

Until late 2022, inflation was primarily driven by energy and food prices. Currently, price increases are predominantly driven by rising services and energy prices.

Household Inflation Expectations Remain Elevated

According to the preliminary Michigan survey, US consumer inflation expectations for the next twelve months fell from 4.7% to 4.3% in November. They thus remain significantly above the level prior to the start of the war in Iran (3.4%). The New York Fed reported 3.9% for September. In the euro area, households in September expected inflation of 3.1% for the coming year and 2.9% for the next three years according to the ECB.

  • USA
  • Eurozone

Fed Postpones Return to Inflation Target Once Again

The US Federal Reserve assumes that inflation in the US will not return to the 2% target until 2029. At the beginning of the year, 2028 was still being projected. Since the resurgence of inflation in 2021, the Fed has had to revise its forecast year after year and postpone reaching the target once again.

Monetary Policy & Interest Rates

Interest Rate Markets Expect Further Tightening

Futures markets are pricing in rate hikes of at least 25 basis points for the US Federal Reserve through January 2027. Through July 2027, a total of around 50 basis points of rate tightening is expected. This would bring the key policy rate back close to the level prior to the rate cuts of the past year. For the euro area, further rate hikes totaling a solid 50 basis points are priced in through July 2027, of which around 20 basis points by January 2027. The expected level of interest rates in Europe remains well below that of the US.

  • Fed (US)
  • ECB (Eurozone)

US Real Interest Rates Remain Positive

The real interest rate is the difference between the nominal interest rate and (expected) inflation. While the nominal interest rate can be observed in the market, an estimate must be used for expected inflation. Depending on the chosen approach, the (expected) real interest rate for the US economy currently lies in positive territory between 0.3% and 2.4%. Alternatively, one can observe the yield on inflation-indexed government bonds (TIPS in the US) in the market. For a two-year maturity, the real rate has been positive since April 2022 and currently stands at around 2.2%.

Real Economy

Real Economy

Financial Conditions

USA and Eurozone: High Financing Costs for Companies

Financing costs for companies in both the US and the euro area have eased recently following a noticeable increase. Yields on five-year corporate bonds in both regions edged lower for both AAA and BBB bonds after having increased sharply over the course of the year. Yield spreads between credit rating categories remained largely stable.

  • us_corp_yields
  • ez_corp_yields

Mortgage Rates in the US Remain at a High Level

In the US, interest rates for 30-year mortgages remain at an elevated level and stood at around 7.2% at the end of October. In Germany, by contrast, interest rates for new residential housing loans remained largely stable at around 4.1%. High financing costs continue to dampen demand in the US residential real estate market.

Weaker Credit Impulse Points to Waning Demand

The credit impulse (the change in credit flows to the private sector in percentage points of GDP) is a reliable leading indicator for private demand trends. Most recently, it remained slightly positive in both the US and the euro area, but lost momentum. Because the number of banks reporting increasing willingness to lend is falling noticeably, particularly in the US, this points to an impending negative credit impulse.

  • Eurozone

US Consumption & Labor Market

US New Orders Remain at a High Level

New orders continue to signal solid momentum for the US economy. In October, manufacturing new orders stabilized at a high level of 55.5 points. In the services sector, new orders eased from 59.8 to 55.9 points, but remain clearly in expansionary territory above the 50-point threshold.

US Services Sector Cools Down Slightly

Business conditions in the US services sector are somewhat less dynamic than in the previous month. The Purchasing Managers’ Index declined from 58.8 to 56.1 points, but continues to signal solid expansion and supports the overall outlook together with robust manufacturing order intakes.

US Retail Sales Continue to Rise

Retail sales in the US continue to grow solidly. On a 4-week average, the Johnson Redbook Index stood at a strong 9.8% year-on-year (with weekly peaks of up to 10.4%), pointing to ongoing robust nominal consumption among US households.

US Savings Rate Declines

Between 2000 and 2019, the US household savings rate averaged 5.2%. Following the recent data revision, the savings rate in July was not at 3% as previously assumed, but significantly higher at 4.5%. The savings rate had been underestimated since 2022. The US consumer thus built up more financial cushion than assumed. Recently, however, the savings rate weakened and fell back to 3.8% in September 2026 (following 4.0% in August).

USA: Google Searches Point to More Cautious Consumer Behavior

Consumption often begins with an internet search. Online search data therefore serves well as a leading indicator for private consumption. Search intensity for the term “restaurant” in the US in recent weeks was slightly below the 2021 to 2024 average, having previously been higher. Terms in the “travel” category have been searched somewhat less than the 2021 to 2024 average since March.

  • Restaurant
  • Travel

US Consumer Sentiment Remains Weak

American consumer sentiment remains on average pessimistic. The Conference Board Consumer Confidence Index is at levels seen during the COVID crisis. The University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index remains at levels otherwise only seen during recessions. As usual, supporters of the opposition party are significantly more pessimistic. Republicans, by contrast, remained optimistic, but have become more pessimistic since the outbreak of the war in Iran.

  • Sentiment
  • Partisanship

US Employment Growth Stabilizes at a Moderate Level

In October, non-farm private sector employment rose by 0.6% year-on-year according to the ADP report. Figures from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) showed annual growth of 0.7%, significantly higher than expected. The US labor market remains stable, but job growth is now proceeding more slowly than in the years between the financial and COVID crises.

USA: Layoffs and Initial Jobless Claims Low

In October, announced job cuts according to Challenger remained at a muted level of around 44,000. At the same time, weekly initial jobless claims fell back to around 189,000 at the end of October, continuing to underline the robust condition of the US labor market.

  • Layoffs
  • Initial Claims

The US Labor Market is Less Tight

The Beveridge curve shows the relationship between the job vacancy rate—vacancies as a percentage of total employment plus vacancies—and the unemployment rate. During the COVID crisis, the curve shifted outward because policy measures kept unemployment high despite abundant job openings. These rigidities have dissipated, and the Beveridge curve has returned to pre-COVID levels. The US labor market environment has thus normalized.

USA: Similar Wage Growth Across All Wage Quartiles

In September, average hourly earnings in the US rose by 3.7%. In the lowest wage quartile, they rose by 3.4%, somewhat slower than in the previous month, and remained unchanged at 3.8% in the highest quartile. Overall, wage growth across all four wage quartiles ranges between 3.4% and 4.0%.

Eurozone

Eurozone: Momentum Wanes

In the Eurozone, the services sector supports economic activity, but is losing momentum. At the same time, the manufacturing PMI remained well above the neutral 50-point threshold at 52.7.

Booming Business Conditions in Spain

In Spain and Italy, business conditions remained expansionary in October. In Spain, the Composite PMI stood at a high level of 56.2 points, continuing to be strongly supported by the services sector (57.2). In Italy, momentum slowed moderately to 50.8 points, supported by services (52.0 points), while manufacturing was in contractionary territory at 49.4 points.

  • Spain
  • Italy

Google Searches Point to Consumer Restraint in Germany and France

Google searches for “restaurant” in Germany and France point to weak and declining private demand. In both countries, search intensity in recent weeks was lower than the 2021 to 2024 average for the same time of year.

  • Germany
  • France

Google Searches Paint a More Favorable Picture in Southern Europe

While search intensity for “restaurant” in Spain remained as high as the 2021 to 2024 average, it was slightly higher in Italy.

  • Spain
  • Italy

Eurozone: Unemployment Remains Low Despite Fewer Vacancies

The Beveridge curve, illustrating the relationship between vacancy rate and unemployment rate, points to a tight labor market in the Eurozone. During the COVID crisis, the curve shifted outward as policy measures caused unemployment to remain elevated despite high job vacancies. In the meantime, these rigidities have largely dissipated and unemployment stands at historically low levels. Although the vacancy rate has declined, it once again aligns with the historical relationship between the two variables.

China

China’s Construction Sector Continues to Show No Turnaround

The number of new housing starts remained weak in September as well and has persisted far below 2010 levels since 2022. This is also reflected in the numbers of unfinished and completed properties, which continue to show no sign of a turnaround.

China’s Consumers Remain Pessimistic

In China, consumer sentiment has improved slightly since early 2025, but remains historically low. Sentiment in the real estate sector is likewise depressed. The “National Real Estate Climate Index” has been trending downward for three years. Publication of the index was discontinued in January on the grounds that it no longer accurately represented actual developments in the real estate sector.

China: Very High Youth Unemployment

China’s unemployment rate moved sideways at around 5% in recent years and stood at 5.2% in September. Youth unemployment, defined as the jobless rate among 16- to 24-year-olds, reached 21.3% in mid-2023. Following a six-month publication pause, a new statistic “excluding students” has been available since December 2023. After rising in August, this rate eased slightly to 18.0% in September, but remains at a high level.

China Counters Domestic Weakness with Expansionary Monetary Policy

The Chinese central bank manages its monetary policy primarily through three instruments: the overnight reverse repo rate, the one-year Loan Prime Rate (LPR) of commercial banks, and the reserve requirement ratio (RRR). In November 2026, the overnight rate (1.25%) replaced the previous 7-day rate as the key policy rate for daily liquidity management. In recent years, the PBoC has lowered all benchmark rates multiple times to provide more liquidity and thereby support the economy through credit expansion. For 2026 and 2027, it signals a continued expansionary stance.

Outlook

Outlook

Positive Fiscal Impulses: 2026 in the Euro Area, 2027 in China

The fiscal impulse measures the change in the cyclically adjusted primary deficit as a percentage of GDP (positive values indicate fiscal expansion, negative values restriction—independent of the absolute deficit level). In the euro area, the German defense and investment program in particular provides a positive impulse of +0.6% of GDP in 2026; barely any additional impulse follows for 2027 (+0.03%). In China, the impulse in 2026 is moderately positive at +0.26% and strengthens to +0.32% in 2027. In the US, by contrast, fiscal policy turns into a contractionary impulse in 2027 (-0.24%).

Our growth forecasts are based on a qualitative assessment of economic momentum, the growth effect of fiscal policy, and financing conditions. Directional guidance, no point forecasts. Consensus: Bloomberg.

USA: The improvement in business conditions in conjunction with stable private consumption and a solid labor market underpins the positive momentum. AI developments support growth through a positive wealth effect on private consumption. On the fiscal side, no budget consolidation is planned. The negative fiscal impulse justifies our overall neutral assessment of fiscal policy. Monetarily, multiple interest rate hikes are priced in through mid-2027, which tightens financing conditions.

China: The persistent weakness in the real estate sector weighs on domestic demand. Manufacturing stabilizes the economy via exports, albeit particularly via subsidies, which points to overcapacity and has a deflationary effect. Slightly positive impulses can be expected from fiscal and monetary policy.

Eurozone / Germany: A recovery in Germany supports momentum in the Eurozone, while France remains weak. Spain and Italy contribute to growth, with the services sector providing the main contribution. Debt-financed economic stimulus programs, particularly in Germany, should provide an impulse through late 2026. Without deregulation, however, German infrastructure and defense investments are likely to have only a weak effect. Elevated energy prices (especially for natural gas) and more restrictive financing conditions place an additional burden on the economy. We have slightly raised our forecast for Germany to 0.7%, while for the Eurozone it remains at 0.6%.
