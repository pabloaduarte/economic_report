Macro Outlook in Brief

Global: Uncertainty Weighs on Growth The recent escalation in the Iran conflict is driving uncertainty up again and is likely to weigh on the recovery of the business situation.

USA: Robust but Cautious Consumption The labor market is surprisingly strong and consumption remains solid. If inflation remains persistent, interest rate increases could weaken consumption and dampen growth.

Eurozone: Headwinds from Energy Prices and Interest Rates Rising oil and gas prices as well as higher key interest rates weigh on credit demand and the economy.

China: Policy Supports a Weak Domestic Economy Expansionary monetary and fiscal policies stabilize the economy, but weak domestic demand and geopolitical risks slow growth.

Recovery in Offside?

REAL ECONOMY

Growth

USA and Eurozone: Short-Lived Recovery?

In May, the business situation in the US remained positive, while it clouded over further in the Eurozone. The Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) measures the current business situation and companies’ short-term expectations. It is above 50 if more companies report an improvement than a deterioration. In the Eurozone, the PMI fell to 48.5, as the services sector PMI in particular fell sharply. In the US, it fell slightly to 51.5, while the renewed blockade clouds the outlook again.

Weak Services Sector

  • USA
  • Eurozone

The services sector in the Eurozone recovered slightly in June. The services PMI rose to 49.4, but remains in contractionary territory. The manufacturing PMI fell slightly to 51.4. In the US, the services sector rose with a PMI of 51.2, while industry continued to expand significantly at 53.9. Another rise in oil prices and uncertainty over the duration of the war in Iran are likely to worsen business prospects, particularly in the Eurozone, in the coming months.

Germany and France with Gloomy Outlook

  • Germany
  • France

In Germany and France, the business situation has clouded over further. The services PMI in May was 48.8 in Germany and 44.3 in France. The manufacturing PMIs also declined. Whether they will increase again in the coming month is likely to continue to depend on the development of energy prices.

China: Weak Domestic Economy

The purchasing managers’ indices for May 2026 paint a mixed picture. The official NBS PMI, which primarily tracks larger and domestic-oriented companies, was 50.0 in industry and 50.3 in the services sector. In contrast, the private, more export-oriented RatingDog PMI remained higher and more volatile at 51.8 (industry) and 54.4 (services). The divergence continues to reflect the weakness of the domestic market compared to the export economy.

Construction Activity Not Recovering

The number of new building starts fell further in March and has been below the 2010 level since 2022. This is also reflected in the number of unfinished and completed properties, which also fell sharply.

Private Consumption

US: Retail Sales Continue to Rise

Retail sales rose by 7.5% year-on-year in May. The weekly ‘Johnson Redbook Retail Sales’ index, which tracks sales of department stores and retail outlets, rose sharply and signals strong growth in US retail sales through mid-July.

US: Credit Card Debt Rising Slightly Slower

US credit card debt fell slightly in June, but remains high. Since mid-2021, credit card debt has been rising as fast as on average since 2012.

US: Google Searches Point to More Cautious Consumption

  • Restaurants
  • Travel

Consumption often begins with an internet search. Therefore, online search data is a good leading indicator for private consumption. Search intensity for the term ‘restaurant’ has been slightly below the average of the last five years in recent weeks, after being higher before. Terms in the ‘travel’ category have been searched less than the average of the last five years for two weeks, despite the soccer World Cup.

US: The US Savings Rate Remains Low

The US savings rate fell slightly in June and remained significantly below the historical average of 5.2%. In contrast to the Eurozone, US households have largely depleted the excess savings accumulated during the pandemic. At the same time, real disposable income growth has slowed.

Only Republican Sentiment Remains Optimistic

  • Consumer Confidence
  • Partisan Preference

On average, American consumer sentiment remains pessimistic. The Conference Board’s Consumer Confidence Index is at the level of the Corona crisis. The University of Michigan’s Consumer Sentiment Index reached values that usually only occur in recessions. As usual, supporters of the opposition party are more pessimistic. Republicans, on the other hand, remain optimistic.

Energy Spending: US Lower than Eurozone

Historically, household spending on energy in the US was higher than in Europe. With the Covid crisis and the war in Ukraine, however, this relationship has reversed. Until the end of 2024, the share of energy spending in total household spending in Germany and France was higher than in the US. The current oil price shock is likely to reinforce this trend further.

Eurozone & Germany: Household Savings Rate Remains Stable

In contrast to the US, the household savings rate in the Eurozone remained stable and slightly above the historical average. German savings rate also higher than in the rest of the Eurozone.

Google Searches: Consumer Restraint in France and Germany

  • Germany
  • France

Google searches for ‘restaurant’ in Germany and France point to weak and declining private demand. In both countries, search intensity in recent weeks was lower than the average of the last five years for the same season.

Less Caution in Spain than in Italy

  • Spain
  • Italy

While in Spain search intensity for ‘restaurant’ remained as high as the average of recent years, it was slightly lower in Italy.

China: Sentiment Hardly Improving

In China, consumer confidence recovered slightly in May, but remains historically low. Sentiment in the real estate sector is also depressed. The ‘National Real Estate Climate Index’ has been declining for three years.

Labor Market

Nominal Wages Rising Slower

In June, average hourly earnings in the US rose by 3.6% year-on-year. In the first quarter of 2026, wage growth was 3.9% in Germany and only 1.6% in France. In Germany and the US, wage growth remains above the 2011-2019 average.

US: Wage Growth Normalizing?

Wage growth according to the Indeed Hiring Lab Wage Tracker appears to lead US median wage growth by about seven months. In June, wages posted in job openings in the US rose by 2.4%, indicating a stabilization at this level.

US: Wage Growth Convergence

In June, hourly wages in the US rose by an average of 3.8%. Wage gains across different wage groups are converging: wages in the lowest wage quartile rose by 3.6%, while the highest quartile rose by 3.9%. As a result, the highest wages are no longer growing much faster than during the peak in 2022.

The US Labor Market is Less Tense

The Beveridge curve shows the relationship between the vacancy rate – job openings as a proportion of the sum of filled and open positions – and the unemployment rate. With the Corona crisis, the curve shifted outwards because unemployment remained high despite many vacancies due to policy measures. Rigidities have eased again and the Beveridge curve has returned to its pre-Corona level. The situation on the US labor market has therefore normalized.

Job Growth Recovers, but Remains Weak

In June, the number of private sector non-farm jobs rose by 0.5% year-on-year according to the ADP report. The official BLS figures also showed annual growth of 0.6%. The US labor market remains stable, but job growth is slower than in the years between the financial and COVID crises.

USA: Layoffs and Jobless Claims Within Normal Ranges

  • Layoffs
  • Initial Claims

Around 97,000 job cuts were announced in May, slightly more than in the previous month. At the same time, weekly initial jobless claims recently rose slightly to 225,000, but remained at the lower end of the fluctuations of the past three years and at a historically low level. Between 1990 and 2019, the weekly average was around 350,000.

Nominal Wages Rise Faster in Germany

The Indeed Hiring Lab Wage Tracker shows nominal wages continuing to rise in the Eurozone. In May, the rate of change was 2.4% in the Eurozone, 2.9% in Germany, 1.2% in France, and 2.40% in the US. Only in Germany are wages rising more slowly than in previous months, but they remain at a high level.

Eurozone: Tight Labor Market

The Beveridge curve, which shows the relationship between the vacancy rate and the unemployment rate, points to a tight labor market in the Eurozone. During the Covid crisis, the curve shifted outwards because political measures meant that unemployment remained high despite many job vacancies. In the meantime, these rigidities have largely been reduced and unemployment is at a historically low level. Although the vacancy rate has fallen, it again corresponds to the usual relationship between the two variables.

China: Very High Youth Unemployment

China’s unemployment rate moved sideways at 5% in recent months. Youth unemployment (excluding students) fell slightly to 15.6% in May.

PRICES

Commodities

Sea Freight Costs for Crude Oil and Containers Rise

Freight rates for crude oil tankers have risen to new record levels due to the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. Despite a recent decline, they remain above the peak levels of 2022 following the start of the war in Ukraine. The World Container Index (WCI), which tracks freight rates in merchandise trade, initially remained at a low level due to ongoing overcapacities in container shipbuilding, but has risen sharply in recent weeks.

Backwardation: Declining Oil Prices Expected Again

  • Forward Curve
  • Event Study

Oil markets had expected oil prices to stabilize after the announced reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. The difference between futures prices for oil delivery in three months and in one year has recently risen again due to continued uncertainty about the blockade. This ‘Backwardation’ signals that the current shortage is viewed as temporary. The prices of futures with delivery in one year have risen sharply again in recent days, more than after the start of the war in Ukraine in 2022.

When Will Shipping Through the Strait of Hormuz Normalize?

A reopening of the Strait of Hormuz is crucial for oil supply. Following the renewed attacks, prediction markets price in a reopening by the end of July with only about a 3% probability. For the end of December, the implied probability is 62%.

European Gas Prices Follow a Similar Path to 2022

Europe remains heavily dependent on imports of oil and gas. After a rapid increase of around 75%, Dutch TTF-Erdgas-Futures with a one-month maturity have recently declined slightly, but remain about 50% above the level of a week before the start of the Iran war. In the 2022 energy crisis, European gas prices had temporarily tripled.

USA

US: Inflation Rate Rises Sharply

  • Head CPI
  • Core CPI

In June, consumer prices in the US rose less sharply than in the previous month. The Consumer Price Index (CPI) increased by 3.5% year-on-year, less than expected. Core inflation was 2.6%, below the median expectations of participants in a monthly Bloomberg survey. The continued sharp rise in the price level is particularly, but not only, due to the oil price shock.

Different Indices Show High Inflation

Inflation is determined using different indices. The CPI measures consumer prices, the PCE index measures household spending, and the GDP deflator measures the prices of all domestically produced economic goods. The rates of change of all three indices reached a peak in mid-2022. In May 2026, the inflation rate of the Fed’s favored Core PCE index (excluding energy and food) was 3.3%.

Fed Forecasts vs. Reality

For years, the Federal Reserve underestimated the persistence of inflation in its projections (Summary of Economic Projections - SEP). The chart shows actual Core PCE inflation compared to the median forecast paths from various meeting dates. While the central bank assumed a rapid return to the 2% target starting in June 2021, inflation continued to rise. Only the most recent forecasts reflect the sticky nature of core inflation.

Inflation Drivers: Services and Energy

Inflation in services continues to be the dominant driver of US inflation. While the contribution of food prices remains moderately positive, the recent oil price shock continues to have a strong impact: the inflation contribution of energy prices is higher than it has been since 2022 and is driving the overall rate up noticeably again.

US: Rising Inflationary Pressure

In June, service prices rose by 3.2% year-on-year. Food prices continued to rise strongly with an annual rate of change of 2.7%. Energy prices rose particularly sharply by 15.5%.

US: Supercore Inflation Rises Again

Supercore is defined as the services price index excluding energy and housing costs. Based on the CPI index, the annual rate of Supercore inflation in June was around 3.2%. Measured by the PCE index, Supercore inflation rose to just under 3.9% in May. This shows that inflation pressure in the services sector remains high even without energy and housing costs.

Rent Growth No Longer Falling

Housing costs make up a large part of service prices in the CPI and are also strongly correlated with owner-occupied housing costs. During the Covid years, the Zillow rent index for new leases was a good leading indicator of rent trends, as existing rents react more sluggishly. Currently, the Zillow rent index with around 1.9% signals slower rent growth than the rebounding CPI rent index with around 3.1%.

US: Inflation Could Rise Further

The price index in the ISM Services PMI leads consumer price inflation by about six months. In June, the ISM price index fell slightly, but remains at an elevated level.

Eurozone

Rising Inflation in the Eurozone

  • HICP & Core
  • Country Comparison

In May, the Eurozone HICP rose by 3.2% year-on-year, core inflation (excluding energy and food) was 2.5%. In Germany (2.7%), France (2.8%), Italy (3.3%) and Spain (3.6%), inflation in May was again clearly above the 2% target.

Price Increases in Services and Energy

Until the end of 2022, energy and food prices were the main contributors to inflation. Currently, price increases are predominantly driven by rising service prices, and to a lesser extent by food and again by energy. In the meantime, however, energy prices had a dampening effect on inflation for several months.

Inflation Expectations

Volatile Consumer Inflation Expectations

  • USA
  • Eurozone

In May 2026, US consumers’ inflation expectations for the next twelve months were 4.8% (University of Michigan) and 3.6% (New York Fed). In the Eurozone, households expected a median of 4% for the coming year and 2.9% for the next three years in February, according to the ECB.

Energy Becomes More Expensive Again …

  • USA
  • Eurozone

Volatile food and energy prices are excluded to isolate the underlying development of inflation. However, it is precisely these prices that are crucial for the perception of purchasing power and voting behavior. After the sharp rise in 2021, energy prices have stabilized, but food prices continue to rise. The war in Iran has now driven energy costs up again: in the US, they are 24% above the level before Trump’s election (Nov. 2024), while in the Eurozone, they are 7%.

… Which is Likely to Weigh on the Political Mood

In November 2026, the entire House of Representatives and 35 of the 100 Senate seats will be up for election. In the prediction markets, a Republican majority in the House of Representatives is currently priced in at only 17%. For the Senate, the probability has recently risen again and currently stands at around 56%.

China: Deflation Concerns

The Chinese inflation rate has been below 1% since mid-2023. In April it was 1.2%, core inflation (excluding food and energy) was 1.2%. This underscores the country’s weak economic dynamic. High oil and energy prices are likely to increase the inflation rate at least temporarily.

INTEREST RATE EXPECTATIONS
& FISCAL IMPULSE

US: Rising Real Interest Rate

The real interest rate is the difference between the nominal rate and (expected) inflation. While the nominal interest rate can be read from the market, an estimate must be used for expected inflation. Depending on the chosen approach, the (expected) real interest rate for the US economy currently lies in positive territory between 0.3% and 1.5%. Alternatively, the yield on inflation-indexed government bonds (TIPS in the US) can be read from the market. For a maturity of two years, the real interest rate has been positive since April 2022 and recently rose to around 2.1%.

Volatile Interest Rate Expectations

  • Fed (USA)
  • ECB (Eurozone)

The futures markets are pricing in at least one 25 basis point rate hike by the Fed by December 2026, and at most one more 25 basis point hike by July 2027. In the Eurozone, further interest rate hikes totaling 50 basis points are priced in by July 2027, with 25 basis points already expected by the end of 2026. The expected interest rate level in Europe nevertheless remains significantly below that of the US.

China: Expansive Monetary Policy

The Chinese central bank manages its monetary policy primarily through three instruments: the 7-day reverse repo rate, the one-year Loan Prime Rate (LPR) of commercial banks and the Reserve Requirement Ratio (RRR). In recent years, it has lowered all three rates several times to provide more liquidity and thus support the economy via credit expansion. For 2026, it signals a continued moderately loose monetary policy.

Moderate Fiscal Impulses for 2026

The latest IMF estimates have changed the picture for China in 2026 in particular. Instead of a previously expected neutral fiscal impulse, a strong positive impulse is now projected, driven by extensive government measures to support domestic demand. In the US, the fiscal impulse was negative in 2024 and 2025, but a slight easing is expected for 2026. In the Eurozone, the positive fiscal impulse shifts from 2025 to 2026, as in particular the German armaments and infrastructure program will only take effect with a delay.

Our growth forecast for 2026 is based on a qualitative assessment of economic momentum, the growth effect of fiscal policy, and financing conditions, including monetary policy. These forecasts are to be understood as guidelines and not as precise point predictions.

USA: Stable consumption, a solid labor market situation, and slower wage increases support the unchanged positive momentum. Fiscally, no budget consolidation is planned. Rather, the course remains expansionary. Monetarily, interest rate increases are expected again by the end of the year. The war in Iran increases uncertainty and recession risk.

China: The continuing weakness of the real estate sector weighs on domestic demand. Industry stabilizes the economy via exports, but in particular via subsidies, which points to overcapacities and has a deflationary effect. Slight positive impulses are to be expected from fiscal and monetary policy.

Eurozone / Germany: The negative momentum in Germany and the Eurozone due to the impact of the oil price shock on demand and industry is partially offset at the European level by stronger growth in Spain and Italy. In particular, Spain remains resilient. Debt-financed economic programs are likely to provide an impulse in 2026. Without deregulation, however, German infrastructure and armaments investments are likely to have only a weak effect. Rising oil and gas prices as well as a more restrictive monetary policy weigh additionally on the economy.
